# LifeOS Company Meeting #005 — Business Model, GTM & Launch System

**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**Status:** DECIDED AS HYPOTHESES / VALIDATION REQUIRED  
**Participants:** Product Director, Growth Lead, Revenue Lead, Head of Product, User Research Lead, Launch Ops Lead, Engineering Lead

## 1. Objective

Create the path from validated user value to a paid, launchable product without bloating the MVP.

---

## 2. Business thesis

Users are not paying for "more tasks" or "more AI messages".

The paid value hypothesis is recurring **decision relief + adaptive guidance**:

- less time deciding what to do;
- fewer active commitments competing for attention;
- a clearer next action;
- faster recovery after plans break;
- useful personal learning accumulated over time.

Subscription is viable only if this value repeats weekly.

---

## 3. Beachhead customer

Primary early customer:

**AI-native solo builders, creators and knowledge workers who manage multiple projects/ideas and already use productivity tools but still struggle with prioritization, follow-through and attention overload.**

Early adopters should ideally:
- use AI frequently;
- have ≥ 3 competing projects/commitments;
- already pay for or seriously use productivity software;
- experience the problem weekly;
- be willing to test a new workflow for at least two weeks.

---

## 4. Positioning

### Category
Adaptive personal development / personal operating system.

### Enemy
Not "bad discipline".

The enemy is **unmanaged attention + too many unresolved commitments + static plans that do not adapt to reality**.

### Core promise hypothesis

> **Know what matters. Know what to do next.**

Supporting line:

> LifeOS turns scattered ideas, goals and commitments into a focused direction, one useful next action, and a plan that adapts as you do.

### What we deliberately do not lead with
- habit tracker;
- to-do list;
- journal;
- calendar;
- "AI life coach" alone.

These are crowded frames that understate the product thesis.

---

## 5. Packaging hypothesis

### Free — prove the loop
- Brain Dump / Capture;
- one Current Season;
- basic NOW;
- manual + limited AI Next Actions;
- one active primary project + limited support projects;
- Focus Mode;
- lightweight Daily Close;
- basic Weekly Reset;
- short recent history.

### Pro — compounding personal intelligence
Potential paid value:
- richer AI planning/adaptation;
- long-term memory and confirmed operating preferences;
- behavioral insights and trend history;
- deeper Ask LifeOS;
- more active planning capacity;
- calendar/integration features;
- advanced Weekly Reset;
- configurable rules/Focus Budget;
- data export/backup enhancements;
- higher AI usage.

### Future privacy/power tier
Potential later:
- BYO model/API;
- self-hosted deployment or private data plane if economically supportable;
- automation/agent controls;
- advanced integrations.

No enterprise/team package in MVP.

---

## 6. Pricing experiment

Competitive paid productivity/personal-growth tools currently establish that users can pay roughly low-teens to low-twenties USD per month when daily/weekly value is strong.

We will not simply copy competitor prices.

### Test anchors
During research/beta test reactions to:
- $6/month;
- $10/month;
- $15/month;
- $20/month.

### Initial paid-beta hypothesis
A founder/early-adopter plan around **$8–12/month equivalent** is plausible to test after the core loop shows retention.

### Rule
Do not permanently grandfather a price before AI cost and retention are understood.

---

## 7. Unit economics guardrails

Track from first paid cohort:
- AI cost / active user;
- infrastructure cost / active user;
- paid conversion;
- monthly churn;
- gross margin;
- support time per paid user.

Target direction, not promise:
- keep variable AI/infrastructure cost comfortably below subscription revenue;
- avoid unlimited expensive AI calls in low-price tiers;
- use smaller/cheaper models for extraction/classification when quality permits;
- reserve stronger reasoning models for high-value planning/review operations.

---

## 8. Distribution strategy

Do not depend on one launch day.

### Channel 1 — Founder-led problem content
Publish material around:
- too many projects;
- why to-do lists do not solve direction;
- focus budget;
- project abandonment;
- planning vs actual capacity;
- "what not to do";
- building a personal operating system.

Every piece should demonstrate a real LifeOS concept and capture interested users.

### Channel 2 — Build in public
Share:
- prototype decisions;
- before/after workflows;
- measured dogfood insights;
- weekly product changes;
- research learnings without exposing private user data.

### Channel 3 — Direct early-adopter recruitment
Reach relevant builders/creators/knowledge workers through communities and direct outreach.

Goal is conversations and active testers, not vanity waitlist size.

### Channel 4 — Product communities
After the product is usable:
- Product Hunt;
- maker/indie communities;
- relevant technical/product communities where self-promotion is allowed;
- targeted Reddit/community posts that lead with learning/usefulness rather than spam.

### Channel 5 — Search / evergreen content
Longer-term topics:
- personal operating system;
- how to prioritize multiple projects;
- AI daily planner vs LifeOS;
- how to stop abandoning projects;
- weekly reset systems;
- brain dump workflow;
- focus budget.

### Channel 6 — Referral loop
Only after users get real value.
Potential prompt:
"Know someone with too many projects? Give them an invite."

No coercive referral gating.

## 9. Launch ladder

### Stage 0 — Founder dogfood
Users: core team / owner.

Required before exit:
- use LifeOS for real daily decisions;
- at least 2 weekly cycles;
- identify obvious workflow breaks;
- event instrumentation works.

### Stage 1 — Design partner alpha
Users: ~5–10.

High-touch onboarding.

Exit signals:
- users can reach a useful Next Action without founder explaining every screen;
- qualitative clarity improvement;
- repeated NOW usage;
- no severe trust/privacy confusion.

### Stage 2 — Private beta
Users: ~30–50.

Exit signals to define empirically, but track:
- onboarding activation;
- first Focus Session;
- Week 1 return;
- Weekly Reset completion;
- recommendation acceptance/edit/rejection;
- meaningful progress days.

### Stage 3 — Paid beta
Introduce pricing to a subset/new users.

Goal:
- prove some users will pay for the recurring loop;
- measure AI cost and support load;
- learn which feature actually drives upgrade.

### Stage 4 — Public beta
Users: hundreds, controlled ramp.

Requirements:
- stable onboarding;
- support docs;
- error monitoring;
- privacy/export/delete flows;
- billing;
- usage limits;
- product analytics;
- backup/recovery;
- AI evaluation gates.

### Stage 5 — Public launch
Launch only when product is live and useful.

Launch assets:
- landing page;
- 60–90 second product demo;
- interactive screenshots/GIFs;
- founder story/problem narrative;
- pricing page;
- privacy/security page;
- public roadmap/feedback path;
- onboarding examples;
- support/FAQ.

Product Hunt can be a distribution event, but not the product strategy.

### Stage 6 — Revenue / retention optimization
Focus on:
- activation;
- D7/D30 retention;
- paid conversion;
- churn reasons;
- recommendation quality;
- AI cost;
- repeat Weekly Reset value.

## 10. Funnel

```text
Problem content / referral / community
              ↓
          Landing page
              ↓
         Try / Waitlist
              ↓
          Brain Dump
              ↓
        Direction clarity
              ↓
         Next Action
              ↓
        First Focus Session
              ↓
         Return tomorrow
              ↓
         Weekly Reset
              ↓
     Recognized repeated value
              ↓
             Pro
```

The main funnel leak to study is not signup → signup-complete. It is **clarity → actual action → return**.

---

## 11. Activation definition

An activated user has completed:

1. Brain Dump;
2. confirmed a Direction/Season;
3. accepted or authored a Next Action;
4. started/completed a Focus Session;
5. returned for at least one later NOW check or Daily Close.

This is stronger than "created 3 tasks".

---

## 12. Revenue milestones

### Milestone R0 — Evidence
Real interviews + prototype usage.

### Milestone R1 — First payment
At least one user pays without being a friend/favor purchase.

### Milestone R2 — Paid cohort
10+ paying users with measured weekly usage.

### Milestone R3 — Repeatable early revenue
50+ paying users and clear top upgrade reason.

### Milestone R4 — Retention proof
Enough cohort history to identify durable retention/churn patterns.

### Milestone R5 — Scalable acquisition experiment
At least one channel repeatedly produces activated users at acceptable effort/cost.

MRR targets come after retention evidence; otherwise revenue can be misleading.

## 14. Product Hunt strategy note

Current Product Hunt guidance emphasizes having a live product, being useful/novel/high-craft, preparing launch assets and participating in the community rather than treating the leaderboard as guaranteed distribution.

Decision:
- do not launch a waitlist-only LifeOS on Product Hunt;
- launch when users can actually try the complete core loop;
- build community presence before launch;
- use feedback/testimonials from private beta to sharpen the launch story.

---

## 15. Growth experiments before public launch

1. Landing page message A/B: "Know what matters" vs "Personal GPS".
2. Interactive prototype signup conversion.
3. 15–20 problem interviews.
4. 5–10 design-partner alpha.
5. Founder-led weekly build log.
6. Direct outreach test: can we recruit 20 qualified testers without paid ads?
7. Paid-beta price anchor test.
8. Referral ask only after a user completes ≥ 2 meaningful weekly cycles.

---

## 16. Decisions made

1. Subscription remains primary business model hypothesis.
2. Free tier proves the loop; Pro monetizes compounding intelligence/adaptation.
3. Early paid test range centers around ~$8–12/month equivalent but is not final.
4. No enterprise/team distraction during MVP.
5. Launch is a staged ladder, not one event.
6. Distribution validation begins before production is complete.
7. Product Hunt is one launch channel, not a dependency.
8. First revenue goal is paid evidence + retention, not vanity MRR.
9. Founder-led content and direct design-partner recruitment are first acquisition channels.
10. Public launch requires a live core loop, privacy controls, billing, analytics and reliability.
