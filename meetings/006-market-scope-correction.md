# LifeOS Company Meeting #006 — Market Scope Correction

**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**Status:** BOARD DIRECTIVE / SUPERSEDES NARROW BEACHHEAD ASSUMPTION  
**Participants:** Product Owner, Product Director, Head of Product, User Research, Growth, Revenue, UX

## 1. Correction from Product Owner

LifeOS must serve a **diverse customer base**. The earlier emphasis on "AI-native solo builders / creators / knowledge workers with many projects and ideas" is too narrow if interpreted as the product's target market.

That group may remain useful as one early research or launch cohort because it is easy to reach and has obvious pain, but it **must not define the product architecture, brand, onboarding, or long-term market**.

---

## 2. Revised market thesis

LifeOS targets a broad class of people who want to improve their lives but struggle with one or more of these recurring problems:

1. **No clear direction** — "I don't know what I really want or what goal to choose."
2. **Know the goal but don't act** — "I know what I should do, but I keep delaying it."
3. **Start and abandon** — "I get excited, begin, then stop midway."
4. **Information / idea overload** — "Too many thoughts, inputs, tabs, notes, plans and options."
5. **Daily uncertainty** — "I wake up and don't know what I should do today."
6. **Overcommitment** — "I plan more than my real time/energy allows."
7. **Poor self-understanding** — "I repeat patterns but don't see why."
8. **Life transition** — "My priorities changed and my old system no longer fits."
9. **Inconsistent personal growth** — "I consume advice but don't turn it into sustained change."
10. **Fragmented tools** — "My goals, tasks, journal, calendar, habits and learning live in separate systems."

The market is defined by **jobs and pain states**, not by whether the person uses AI or runs many projects.

---

## 3. Core customer archetypes

These archetypes are intentionally broad. They describe different entry points into the same LifeOS kernel.

### A. The Direction Seeker

**Problem:** Unsure what matters or which life direction to choose.

Typical statements:
- "I don't know what goal I should set."
- "I feel like I'm moving but not toward anything."
- "There are many things I could do, but I can't choose."

LifeOS entry journey:

```text
Life Check-in
→ Brain Dump
→ Values / constraints / responsibilities
→ Direction exploration
→ Current Season
→ First Next Action
```

Primary value: **clarity**.

---

### B. The Inconsistent Executor

**Problem:** Has goals but repeatedly fails to follow through.

Typical statements:
- "I make a plan and stop after a few days."
- "I procrastinate even when I know the task."
- "My goals look good on paper but don't become behavior."

LifeOS entry journey:

```text
Existing goal
→ Friction diagnosis
→ Reduce scope
→ Next Action
→ Focus
→ Reflection
→ Adaptive plan
```

Primary value: **follow-through**.

---

### C. The Overloaded Mind

**Problem:** Too many thoughts, inputs, ideas, commitments or unresolved items.

Typical statements:
- "My head is full."
- "I save everything but can't find what matters."
- "Every new idea distracts me from the previous one."

LifeOS entry journey:

```text
Brain Dump
→ AI organization
→ Active vs Incubator
→ Focus Budget
→ NOW
```

Primary value: **mental clarity + attention protection**.

---

### D. The Daily Drifter

**Problem:** Does not know how to turn broad life intentions into a meaningful day.

Typical statements:
- "I have free time but waste it deciding what to do."
- "I end the day busy but not sure I made progress."

LifeOS entry journey:

```text
Morning / opening check-in
→ capacity + context
→ one recommended Next Action
→ Focus
→ Daily Close
```

Primary value: **daily guidance**.

---

### E. The Life Rebalancer

**Problem:** Work, family, health, money or learning has become unbalanced.

Typical statements:
- "One part of my life consumes everything."
- "My priorities changed but my habits and plans didn't."

LifeOS entry journey:

```text
Life snapshot
→ imbalance / constraints
→ Current Season
→ Maintain / Improve / Defer
→ realistic weekly allocation
```

Primary value: **balance and intentional trade-offs**.

---

### F. The Self-Improvement Consumer

**Problem:** Consumes books, videos, courses and advice but converts little into sustained behavior.

Typical statements:
- "I know a lot, but I don't apply it."
- "Every book gives me another system."

LifeOS entry journey:

```text
Capture learning
→ extract applicable principle
→ connect to current Direction
→ experiment / action
→ observe result
→ keep or discard
```

Primary value: **turn knowledge into practice**.

---

### G. The Project / Opportunity Juggler

**Problem:** Multiple projects, side hustles, business ideas or creative opportunities compete for attention.

This is the previously over-emphasized segment.

LifeOS entry journey:

```text
Project inventory
→ compare importance / urgency / evidence
→ Focus Budget
→ Current Season
→ Incubator
→ Next Action
```

Primary value: **prioritization and completion**.

---

### H. The Transitioning Person

**Problem:** New school stage, career move, new responsibility, relocation, family change, recovery from a disrupted routine, or another major life transition changes what matters.

LifeOS entry journey:

```text
What changed?
→ new constraints / responsibilities
→ reset old commitments
→ new Season
→ minimum viable routines
→ review more frequently
```

Primary value: **reorientation**.

---

## 4. Product strategy consequence

LifeOS should have **one shared kernel but multiple entry journeys**.

Do not build eight separate products.

```text
                  LIFEOS KERNEL

     Direction · State · Memory · Events
     Outcomes · Actions · Focus · Reflection
     Adaptation · Rules · User Model
                       ▲
                       │
      ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
      │                │                │
 Direction Journey  Execution Journey  Overload Journey
      │                │                │
 Daily Guidance     Rebalance Journey  Learning Journey
```

The onboarding detects the user's current pain state and routes them to the shortest useful journey.

---

## 5. Revised onboarding principle

Do not ask every user the same long questionnaire.

Start with:

> **What do you need help with most right now?**

Possible choices:
- Find direction
- Know what to do today
- Stop procrastinating
- Finish what I start
- Organize my thoughts
- Balance my life
- Build better routines
- Turn learning into action
- I don't know — help me figure it out

The selected need changes onboarding, but all journeys converge on the same LifeOS data model.

## 6. Segmentation dimensions

Customer research should classify participants by multiple dimensions instead of one persona label.

### Problem state
- unclear direction;
- overloaded;
- inconsistent execution;
- daily uncertainty;
- imbalance;
- transition;
- fragmented information.

### Life context
- student;
- early career;
- employee / knowledge worker;
- freelancer / creator;
- founder / entrepreneur;
- parent / caregiver;
- career changer;
- general self-improvement user.

### Self-management maturity
- no system;
- simple notes/calendar;
- task-manager user;
- advanced productivity-system user.

### AI comfort
- does not use AI;
- occasional AI user;
- frequent AI user;
- AI-native power user.

**AI comfort is a dimension, not the market definition.**

## 7. Research sampling correction

The first 20–30 research participants should be deliberately mixed.

Suggested sample:
- 5–6 direction seekers;
- 5–6 inconsistent executors;
- 5–6 overloaded / multi-commitment users;
- 4–5 users in life transition or rebalance;
- 4–5 general self-improvement users.

Overlap is expected.

Recruit across different life contexts where feasible.

The goal is to discover:
1. which pain states share the same underlying loop;
2. which entry journeys differ;
3. which segments show the strongest retention and willingness to pay;
4. which segments require specialized features that should *not* be in the initial product.

---

## 8. Monetization implication

Different segments may pay for different value:

| Need | Likely paid value hypothesis |
|---|---|
| Direction | deeper guided planning, memory, periodic life reviews |
| Execution | adaptive plans, progress patterns, calendar integration |
| Overload | intelligent capture, organization, search, Incubator |
| Daily guidance | personalized NOW, capacity-aware recommendations |
| Rebalance | life reviews, trend history, scenario planning |
| Learning → action | knowledge-to-experiment workflows and memory |
| Multi-project | Focus Budget, project intelligence, advanced reviews |

The company should eventually discover whether one Pro package is sufficient or whether use-case-based packages are needed.

Do not create complex packaging before retention data exists.

---

## 9. Brand implication

LifeOS should communicate broadly enough that a person does **not** need to identify as a founder, creator, productivity geek or AI user.

Preferred product-level promise remains:

> **Know what matters. Know what to do next.**

This works across multiple segments.

Supporting narrative:

> LifeOS helps you understand where you are, choose what matters now, turn it into realistic action, and adjust as your life changes.

---

## 10. Decisions that supersede Meeting #002

Meeting #002 remains historical evidence of the first strategy, but the following statements are superseded:

- The "AI-native solo builder / creator / knowledge worker" group is **not the overall target market**.
- It is only one possible early-adopter / research cohort.
- Product scope, UX architecture and brand must support diverse problem states.
- Research must sample multiple segments before locking ICP.

---

## 11. Decisions made

1. Segment by **problem/job**, not by AI usage or profession alone.
2. LifeOS will have one adaptive kernel and multiple onboarding/entry journeys.
3. The product must work for users who do not use AI products today.
4. AI remains an enabling technology, not a prerequisite identity.
5. Initial research expands from a narrow cohort to a mixed 20–30 participant sample.
6. We will identify an initial commercial ICP from observed retention/willingness-to-pay, not assume it upfront.
7. The brand remains broad personal-development infrastructure rather than a founder productivity tool.
