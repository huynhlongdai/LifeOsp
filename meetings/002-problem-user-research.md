# LifeOS Company Meeting #002 — Problem & User Research

**Date:** 2026-08-25  
**Status:** DECIDED / VALIDATION REQUIRED  
**Participants:** Product Director, Head of Product, User Research Lead, Behavioral Science Lead, Growth Lead, Revenue Lead

## 1. Meeting objective

Choose the first user segment and turn the product thesis into falsifiable research hypotheses before building.

---

## 2. Market observation

The productivity/personal-growth market already contains strong solutions for pieces of the problem:

- Motion: automatic task prioritization, capacity-aware calendar planning and continuous re-planning.
- Reclaim: AI/agentic scheduling for focus time, tasks, habits, meetings and calendar trade-offs.
- Sunsama: deliberate daily planning and calm professional workflow.
- Tiimo: brain dump → structured tasks, time estimates, visual planning and flexible re-planning.
- Rosebud: AI-assisted reflection, journaling and long-term personal context.
- Mindsera: AI journaling, recurring-topic/emotion analysis and personalized suggestions.

**Strategic conclusion:** "AI planner" is not enough differentiation.

LifeOS must test a broader closed-loop value proposition:

> **clarify direction → protect limited attention → choose next action → execute → reflect → adapt**

The opportunity is a hypothesis, not a proven market gap.

---

## 3. Candidate user segments

### Segment A — AI-native solo builders / creators / knowledge workers
Characteristics:
- several concurrent projects and ideas;
- frequently use Notion/Todoist/Calendar/ChatGPT or similar tools;
- struggle more with prioritization and finishing than with capturing tasks;
- comfortable delegating planning/research to AI;
- potentially willing to pay for daily decision relief.

### Segment B — General self-improvement users who feel directionless
Characteristics:
- strong emotional need;
- huge market;
- less clear willingness to pay;
- onboarding must do much more discovery and education.

### Segment C — Students / early-career users
Characteristics:
- high planning and direction needs;
- possible strong retention if useful;
- lower willingness to pay;
- requires age-appropriate UX and privacy considerations.

### Segment D — Neurodivergent / executive-function-support users
Characteristics:
- strong problem intensity;
- existing competitors specifically address this segment;
- requires careful evidence-based positioning and avoids diagnostic/medical claims.

### Segment E — Founders / executives
Characteristics:
- high willingness to pay;
- overloaded calendars/projects;
- competitors already strong on work scheduling;
- opportunity may be whole-life prioritization rather than calendar optimization.

---

## 4. Beachhead decision

### Primary validation segment
**AI-native solo builders, creators and knowledge workers with project/idea overload.**

Why:
1. the pain is frequent and concrete;
2. the group is reachable online;
3. they already understand productivity software;
4. they can compare LifeOS against existing workarounds;
5. willingness-to-pay can be tested early;
6. LifeOS can later expand to broader personal-development audiences.

### Important rule
We are choosing a beachhead for validation, not permanently excluding other users.

## 5. Core JTBD tree

### Functional job
When I have too many ideas, projects and obligations competing for attention, help me decide what deserves focus, convert that direction into a realistic next action and keep adapting when reality differs from the plan.

### Sub-jobs

#### A. Empty my head
- capture thoughts quickly;
- avoid manual categorization;
- know nothing important will be lost.

#### B. Understand my current situation
- see active commitments;
- identify unfinished/blocked work;
- understand available time and attention;
- separate urgent from meaningful.

#### C. Choose a direction
- compare competing opportunities;
- understand trade-offs;
- decide what not to pursue now;
- create a Current Season.

#### D. Turn direction into action
- define an Outcome;
- identify bottleneck;
- generate the smallest useful Next Action;
- estimate realistic effort.

#### E. Start and stay focused
- reduce context switching;
- capture distractions without changing focus;
- make success conditions visible.

#### F. Recover when I drift
- detect repeated delays;
- diagnose why;
- resize, replace, defer or drop commitments;
- recalculate the plan.

#### G. Learn how I operate
- identify realistic capacity;
- understand repeated patterns;
- compare planned vs actual behavior;
- correct inaccurate AI assumptions.

## 6. Highest-risk assumptions

The product fails if any of these are false:

### A1 — Users experience decision overload often enough
They must encounter "I do not know what to work on next" repeatedly, not once per quarter.

### A2 — Existing tools do not resolve the underlying problem
Users must still feel overloaded despite having task/calendar/note tools.

### A3 — Users are willing to let AI recommend trade-offs
If users only want storage and never want prioritization advice, the product thesis weakens.

### A4 — "What not to do" creates value
Focus Budget + Incubator must feel relieving rather than restrictive.

### A5 — Users return after the initial clarity session
If Brain Dump/Direction is useful once but NOW/Reflect does not create recurring value, retention fails.

### A6 — Behavioral adaptation beats static planning
Users must perceive value when LifeOS changes recommendations based on actual behavior.

### A7 — Users will provide enough personal context
The product must earn enough trust for useful personalization without demanding excessive setup.

### A8 — Users will pay for repeated decision relief
Users must value the recurring loop enough to support subscription economics.

## 7. Problem interview protocol

Target: first 15–20 interviews before locking MVP scope.

Do not demo LifeOS in the first half of the interview.

### Questions
1. Tell me about the last day you had many things to do but did not know what to start with.
2. What did you do next?
3. Which tools did you open?
4. What was still unresolved after using them?
5. Tell me about the last project you started and did not finish.
6. At what point did momentum disappear?
7. What usually happens when you get a new idea while already working on something?
8. How many meaningful projects are active right now?
9. How do you decide that one project should be paused or abandoned?
10. Tell me about the last time your daily plan became unrealistic.
11. How did you re-plan?
12. What information do you keep in your head because your tools do not understand it?
13. Have you used AI to decide priorities or plan a project? What happened?
14. What advice from an AI would you refuse to accept without explanation?
15. What would make you trust an app with personal goals/history?
16. Which productivity/self-development apps have you paid for in the last year and why?
17. If this problem disappeared tomorrow, what would materially improve?

### Evidence collection
For every interview capture:
- concrete recent event, not general opinion;
- current workaround;
- frequency;
- cost (time, stress, abandoned projects, missed opportunities);
- tools paid for;
- willingness to change behavior;
- privacy concerns;
- quotes;
- observed contradictions.

---

## 8. Evidence scoring

Each assumption gets a score after interviews:

- **0 — unsupported**
- **1 — weak anecdotes**
- **2 — repeated evidence**
- **3 — strong recurring evidence + costly workaround**
- **4 — strong evidence + users already pay/actively seek solution**

MVP prototype work may proceed in parallel, but major engineering should not begin until A1–A5 average at least **2.5/4** and no critical assumption scores 0.

---

## 9. Competitor / alternative frame

We should not only compare against apps.

The real alternatives are:
- task managers;
- calendars;
- note-taking systems;
- journaling apps;
- AI chat assistants;
- paper / whiteboard;
- personal coach / accountability partner;
- doing nothing and keeping plans mentally.

### Differentiation hypothesis

LifeOS should not win by having more features. It should win if users say:

> "Other tools store or schedule what I already decided. LifeOS helps me decide what deserves my limited attention, gets me moving, and learns how to adjust with me."

## 11. Prototype test to run after interviews

Test one interactive flow:

```text
Brain Dump
  → AI organizes
  → user corrects
  → Focus Conflict
  → Direction Session
  → Current Season
  → Next Action
  → NOW
  → Focus
  → Daily Close
```

Measure:
- clarity before/after (1–7);
- confidence in chosen focus (1–7);
- recommendation acceptance/edit/rejection;
- time to Next Action;
- whether the user starts the action;
- whether they return the next day;
- emotional reaction to Incubator/Focus Budget;
- trust response to AI explanations.

---

## 12. Monetization research

Do not ask only "would you pay?"

Collect:
- current paid productivity subscriptions;
- most expensive tool they would replace;
- value of saving 15–30 minutes/day of planning/decision time;
- preference: free + Pro vs paid trial;
- willingness to pay at price anchors around $6, $10, $15 and $20/month;
- willingness to use BYO AI key / self-hosting as a privacy/cost option.

Competitive reference points observed during this meeting include premium personal-productivity/self-reflection offerings roughly in the low-teens to low-twenties USD per month, depending on product and billing cadence. LifeOS pricing must be tested against delivered value, not copied.

## 14. Decisions made

1. Beachhead validation segment = AI-native solo builders / creators / knowledge workers with project and idea overload.
2. LifeOS differentiation cannot be "AI planning" alone.
3. The most important product hypothesis is the closed loop: direction → focus constraint → next action → execution → reflection → adaptation.
4. Incubator and Focus Budget must be explicitly validated.
5. First research batch = 15–20 problem interviews.
6. We will collect behavioral evidence, not preference surveys alone.
7. We will not make medical/diagnostic claims or position LifeOS as treatment.
8. Engineering scope remains provisional until critical assumptions have evidence.
